# BBG000Q02P20_2011-11-14 (MGI, MoneyGram International)

## 1. Verdict

Not a delisting. On 2011-11-14 MoneyGram did a 1-for-8 reverse stock split; the same common stock kept trading under
the same ticker MGI with a new CUSIP (60935Y109 to 60935Y208). The library's exchange-transfer ending is an invented
one, and `ticker_history` is wrongly clipped at 2011-11-14.

## 4. Why this confidence

Inferred.
- Exit kind (no ending): filing (8-K 2011-11-14, Items 5.03/8.01: reverse split, no delisting) plus fails rows.
- Successor: filing (same company, same common stock converted 8 to 1).
- Last trade date: none to give; fails rows show MGI continuing.
- Payout rule: worked out (no exit, so none).
- Terms: filing (1-for-8 ratio).
Not backed by a filing: that MGI stayed on its exchange afterwards. It rests on fails rows (MGI under CUSIP
60935Y208, 59 rows 2011-11-16..2012-04-30, first $18.39, last $16.92) and on the absence of any Form 25 or Form 15
in the window (filings list 2011-08..2012-12). A later 10-K cover page naming the exchange would make it verified.

## 5. What happened

- 2011-11-14: the company filed a charter amendment effecting a 1-for-8 reverse split, effective on filing; every eight
  shares became one ([SEC 8-K 2011-11-14](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1273931/000095012311098121/d85662e8vk.htm)).
- Fails rows: CUSIP 60935Y109 under MGI through 2011-11-14 (last $2.87); CUSIP 60935Y208 under MGI from 2011-11-16
  (first $18.39, about 8 times $2.3) (fails data via sec.py `fails --symbol MGI`).
- No Form 25 or Form 15 in the issuer's filings list from 2011-08-01 to 2012-12-31 (sec.py `filings`); 8-Ks
  continue through 2012 (earnings, 5.02, 5.07).

## 6. Decision-tree bucket

Kept the same security: same issuer, same ticker, same FIGI; only the CUSIP and share count changed.

## 7. Why the library got it wrong

No Form 25 existed, so the finder fell to the continued-filings fallback (304, `no_form25`,
`last_trade_date_unconfirmed`, `delist_date_approx`), which took the last sighting under the old CUSIP (2011-11-14)
as an ending. The CUSIP change from the reverse split made the old CUSIP stop. Cause tag: `continued_filings_guess`.

## 8. Fix and open checks

- Treat a CUSIP change with the same ticker and issuer, and no Form 25, as the same security (no ending); extend
  the CUSIP history to 60935Y208 and keep MGI unclipped.
- Open: confirm the exchange listing continued (10-K FY2011 cover page).
- Golden case: yes, a reverse-split CUSIP change that must yield no ending (note: the library's 2011-11-14 row
  should disappear).

## 9. Verification

Skeptic re-check of the four fields the report calls wrong, from the filings (sec.py `filings`, `grep`; fails rows
via `fails --symbol MGI`). Overall: all four upheld.

- **exit_kind: upheld.** The 8-K of 2011-11-14 (0000950123-11-098121) has Items 5.03/7.01/8.01/9.01 and no 3.01. It
  states a 1-for-8 reverse split of the same common stock, $0.01 par: "every eight shares ... converted ... into one
  share". The 424B3 of the same day (0000950123-11-098144) says "Our common stock is listed on the New York Stock
  Exchange under the symbol MGI", gives the last sale as $2.87 on 2011-11-11 and notes the split. The FY2011 10-K
  (0001193125-12-104763, filed 2012-03-09) lists common stock on the NYSE on its cover. This closes the report's
  open check. The filings list for 2011-10 to 2012-12 shows no Form 25, Form 15, 8-K12B or 8-K12G3. Under decision 9
  and the decision table, a split with the same issuer, ticker and FIGI is not an ending.
- **last_trade_date: upheld.** Fails rows: CUSIP 60935Y109 under MGI, 3 rows 2011-11-01..2011-11-14 (last $2.87);
  60935Y208 under MGI, 59 rows 2011-11-16..2012-04-30 (first $18.39, about 8 x 2.3, last $16.92). The stock traded on.
  The library's published date is blank, so the field is "wrong" only in that it sits on a row that should not exist;
  with the row gone there is nothing to date. The library's internal 2011-11-14 is the last old-CUSIP sighting, not
  an exchange print.
- **value_rule: upheld.** The library says `transfer` (0.0 fill). The right answer is `continuation` (not an exit),
  with no cash, no stock leg and no payout, which follows from the exit_kind finding. A reverse split is not
  consideration for the old share.
- **ticker_history: upheld.** The library clips MGI at 2011-11-14, but the same ticker, issuer (CIK 1273931) and
  common stock traded through at least 2012-04-30 (fails rows), and the 10-K shows an NYSE listing in March 2012.
  The clip is wrong. The new CUSIP 60935Y208 should be added to the CUSIP history.

Minor caveat, not a refutation: the report's "continuation wrong" row in section 2 is a label choice. The cleaner
statement is that there is no ending at all, so continuation does not apply. The verdict is unchanged.
