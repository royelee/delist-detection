# CIK1056087-COMMON_2016-05-25 (MNI, McClatchy Class A)

## 1. Verdict

The 2016-05-25 "exchange transfer" is not an ending. MNI did a 1-for-10 reverse split on 2016-06-07 (new CUSIP 579489303, same ticker), moved from the NYSE to NYSE American in September 2017, and really ended on 2020-02-13, when NYSE American suspended trading after the Chapter 11 filing; the stock then traded OTC as MNIQQ. The library's row is wrong: wrong date, wrong kind (a bankruptcy drop with an OTC-print value), and the ticker_history and cusip_history stop four years early.

## 4. Why this confidence

Inferred.
- Exit kind: filing (8-K 2020-02-14 item 3.01 and the 2020-02-13 8-K item 1.03: Chapter 11, trading suspended; Form 25-NSE 2020-02-21).
- Successor: filing (no successor registration; Form 15 2020-10-02 ends registration).
- Last trade date: worked out from the 8-K ("suspended trading effective immediately" on 2020-02-13); the filing does not say which session was last. Fails rows run under MNI to 2020-02-18 and under MNIQQ from 2020-02-19, which fits.

Fails rows: 579489105 "MNIXXXX" 2016-06-08, then 579489303 under MNI from 2016-06-08 (314 rows to 2020-02-18) (fails rows).

- 2017-09-08 8-A12B (NYSE American registration) and 2017-09-18 Form 25 filed by the issuer under Rule 12d2-2(c), a voluntary withdrawal from the NYSE ([SEC 8-A12B](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056087/000110465917056327/a17-21468_18a12b.htm), [SEC Form 25](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056087/000110465917057627/a17-22133_125.htm)). ## 6. Decision-tree bucket

Lost: bankruptcy, then OTC. The 2016 reverse split (same holders, same claim, a new CUSIP) and the 2017 exchange move are the same security continuing; only the 2020 event ends it.

## 7. Why the library got it wrong

Its reason is the continued-filings fallback (`continued_filings_rule`, CRSP 304, no Form 25, `delist_date_approx`): the observations and fails rows under the old CUSIP 579489105 stop at 2016-05-25, so the finder read the end of that CUSIP as the end of the listing. The reverse split's new CUSIP 579489303 was never joined to the security. Cause: `continued_filings_guess`.

## 8. Fix and open checks

- Follow the CUSIP change at the 2016 reverse split (same issuer, same ticker, fails rows under MNI) so the security continues; then the finder meets the 2020 Form 25-NSE and the 3.01/1.03 filings and classifies a bankruptcy drop. The 2017 Form 25 is an exchange transfer (continuation of the same security, no ending).
- cusip_history should carry 579489303 from 2016-06-07.
- Open: the last session (2020-02-12 vs 02-13) and the first MNIQQ print date, from an exchange or OTC print source; no market price is needed here.
- Golden case: yes, a reverse split with a CUSIP change followed by an exchange move and a bankruptcy drop; well sourced.

## 9. Verification

A skeptic re-opened the cited filings and the fails rows. All four disputed fields stand; one has a one-day caveat.

- **exit_kind: upheld.** The 2016 reverse split is confirmed ([SEC 8-K 2016-06-07](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056087/000114420416107284/v441925_8k.htm): one-for-ten, Class A and B, effective 7:00 a.m. ET 2016-06-07). The CUSIP changed but the ticker stayed MNI. Fails rows show 579489105 under MNI to 2016-05-25 and under MNIXXXX on 2016-06-08, and the new CUSIP 579489303 under MNI from 2016-06-08 to 2016-12-29. The old CUSIP's last rows are therefore a reverse-split artifact, not an ending, and the library's 304 row has no Form 25. The 2017 move is confirmed ([SEC 8-K 2017-09-07](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056087/000110465917055971/a17-21278_18k.htm) item 3.01: voluntary transfer from the NYSE to NYSE American; the 2017-09-18 Form 25 is the issuer's NYSE withdrawal). The real ending is confirmed ([SEC 8-K 2020-02-14](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056087/000105608720000009/mni-20200213x8k.htm) item 3.01: on 2020-02-13 NYSE American suspended trading of the Class A common stock under Company Guide 1003(c)(iii) after the Chapter 11 petition; [SEC 8-K 2020-02-13](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056087/000105608720000005/mni-20200212x8k.htm) item 1.03; [Form 25-NSE](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1056087/000114331320000012/xslF25X02/primary_doc.xml) effective 2020-02-21). A bankruptcy drop with no continuation is the right call. The security class is Class A common in all of these filings.
- **last_trade_date: upheld, with a one-day caveat.** The library's value is blank and its internal guess (2016-05-25) is wrong by almost four years. The 8-K says trading was suspended "effective immediately" on 2020-02-13, the same day as the petition. That does not settle whether 2020-02-13 had any prints or 2020-02-12 was the last session (the reference's before-the-open rule does not apply). Fails rows fit either date (MNI to 2020-02-18, MNIQQ from 2020-02-19). The date stays `inferred`, not exchange-print verified. It is before the Form 25 effective date (2020-02-21), so decision 12 is not breached.
- **value_rule: upheld.** Decision 11 values a bankruptcy drop to OTC at the first off-exchange print. The 2020-02-13 8-K mentions trading on the OTC Pink Market, and fails rows show 579489303 under MNIQQ from 2020-02-19. `transfer` (a 0.0 fill) is wrong for this security. The MNIQQ symbol comes from fails rows, not a filing.
- **ticker_history: upheld.** The library's range ends 2016-05-25 (cusip_history has 579489105 only), but MNI kept trading through 2020-02-18 under CUSIP 579489303, which the library never joined. The correct end is the 2020 suspension (about 2020-02-13).

Nothing was refuted. Still open: which of 2020-02-12 and 2020-02-13 was the last session, and the first MNIQQ print date.
