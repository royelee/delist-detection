## 1. Verdict

A 1:1 holding-company reorganization: Office Depot, Inc. became a subsidiary of The ODP Corporation, and every share converted one for one into ODP Corporation stock, which kept trading on Nasdaq as ODP. This is a continuation. The library's `exit_kind=exchange` is right, but `continuation=false`, the blank successor and the blank last trade date are wrong or missing.

## 9. Verification

Field checked: value_rule (library `transfer`, report `continuation`). Upheld.

- Re-read the 8-K12B (0001193125-20-184552, CIK 800240): ODI merged into OD LLC under a Section 251(g) holdco merger on 2020-06-30 (effective 8:00 p.m. ET). Each ODI common share, on a reverse-split-adjusted basis (1-for-10 reverse split effective 4:01 p.m. the same day, applied to the old line first), "automatically converted into an equivalent corresponding share of ODP Common Stock" with the same rights, and holders became ODP holders. No cash is paid in the exchange. ODP replaced ODI on Nasdaq under the same ticker ODP and is the Rule 12g-3(a) successor issuer.
- reference.md, decision 9 and the decision table: a 1:1 holdco reorganization is a continuation with the successor named, and a continuation has no value (rule `continuation`, not an exit). The library's `transfer` (an exchange move with the same shares, 0.0 fill) does not fit, since the security changed (new CUSIP).
- Fails rows (sec.py fails --cusip 88337F105): ODP Corp COM CUSIP 88337F105 trades from 2020-07-01 (one placeholder row under ODPZZZZ at $1.00, then ODP from 2020-07-02 at about $22.66), consistent with the new line replacing the old one.
- Caveat: the successor's sec_id is not in the run, so `continuation=true` with a link cannot be published until it is added. This affects the fix, not the rule. The only unusual term is the same-day 1-for-10 reverse split, which is a split of the old line, not consideration.
