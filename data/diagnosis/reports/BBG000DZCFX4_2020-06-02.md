# LPI (Laredo Petroleum), BBG000DZCFX4, case BBG000DZCFX4_2020-06-02

## 1. Verdict
Not a delisting. On 2020-06-01 a 1-for-20 reverse stock split changed the CUSIP from 516806106 to 516806205; the same common stock kept trading on the NYSE as "LPI". The library read the CUSIP change as an end (a guessed continued-filings exchange transfer), and it is wrong to publish an ending at all.

## 5. What happened
- The 1-for-20 reverse split took effect at 5:00 p.m. on 2020-06-01; from the open on 2020-06-02 the common stock continued on the NYSE under "LPI" with new CUSIP 516806205 ([SEC 8-K 2020-06-01](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1528129/000152812920000108/a0601208-k.htm)).
- Fails rows: CUSIP 516806106 under LPI 2020-05-01..2020-06-02 at about $0.89; CUSIP 516806205 under LPI 2020-06-04..2020-08-31 at $17.56 to $16.90, same issuer name (fails rows, sec.py fails).
- The issuer's filing list from 2020-03 to 2022-12 shows no Form 25, 25-NSE or Form 15, and it kept filing 8-Ks and results ([SEC filing list](https://www.sec.gov/cgi-bin/browse-edgar?action=getcompany&CIK=1528129)).

## 6. Decision-tree bucket
Kept the same security: same issuer, same ticker, same exchange, only a new CUSIP from a reverse split.

## 7. Why the library got it wrong
Its CUSIP history stops at the old CUSIP on 2020-06-02 and the next CUSIP was not attached to this security, so the run saw the security end and fell to the continued-filings rule (`continued_filings_rule`, flags `no_form25`, `delist_date_approx`, `successor_unknown`). Cause: reverse split CUSIP change read as an ending.

## 8. Fix and open checks
- Drop the ending; extend the CUSIP history with 516806205 from 2020-06-02 and run ticker_history to the real end (the later rename and fate of the line were not checked).
- A reverse split alone must not end a CUSIP when the same issuer and ticker continue (fails rows show the new CUSIP under the same symbol and name).
- Golden case: yes (reverse split with a CUSIP change, no Form 25).

## 9. Verification
Re-checked the 8-K of 2020-06-01 (accession 0001528129-20-000108, via sec.py grep), the Form 25/25-NSE/15/15-12B/15-12G/15F filter of the issuer's filings 2020-05-15..2021-06-30 (no hit), and the fails rows of CUSIP 516806205 (offline). reference.md: a rename or an unchanged listing is not an ending; a CUSIP change alone is not an exit; the continued-filings rule is "often a guess".
- exit_kind: upheld. Item 5.03 says the 1-for-20 split took effect at 5:00 p.m. on 2020-06-01 and that from the open on 2020-06-02 the Common Stock "will continue to trade, on a split-adjusted basis, on the New York Stock Exchange under the symbol LPI", new CUSIP 516806205. No Form 25 or Form 15 in the window, so the library's 304 "exchange" ending has no support.
- last_trade_date: upheld. With no ending there is none; the library's blank is only a flag of a guess (2020-06-02 is the first day on the new CUSIP, not a last trade).
- successor: upheld, with a note. The security itself carries on (same issuer, ticker, exchange, new CUSIP), so no successor to another security exists; the "wrong" label is mainly that the row should not exist.
- value_rule: upheld. A reverse split pays nothing except cash in lieu of fractions (8-K), so the `transfer` rule on an ending is moot.
- ticker_history: upheld. Fails rows show CUSIP 516806205 under LPI from 2020-06-04 to 2020-08-31 (53 rows, $17.56 to $16.90), same issuer name, so LPI runs past 2020-06-02.
Not refuted. Open check unchanged: the later rename and the true end of the LPI line were not checked. Confidence in the verdict: high.
